CONCEPT 1 — HUMAN AMONG SYNTHETIC ECHOES

Narrative meaning:

“Comments that sound human but aren’t.”

Use Phase 14A.1 Direction A / Frame 1 as the main visual reference.

Candidate A:

- One warm human moving naturally across the composition
- Several cool synthetic echoes repeating a nearly identical pose
- The human is clearly distinguishable by warmth, rhythm and anatomy
- Echoes are mechanically similar without becoming literal clones
- Strong lateral movement and generous negative space

Candidate B:

- One warm human standing or moving against a field of cool repeated echoes
- Slightly more asymmetric editorial composition
- Echoes should create visual pressure without surrounding the human like
  a threatening mob
- Human remains the emotional and compositional anchor

Both candidates must communicate:

- Real human rhythm
- Artificial repetition
- Difference without horror
- Recognition rather than fear

CONCEPT 2 — UNCERTAIN DIGITAL REFLECTION

Narrative meaning:

“Videos where the face looks right, but the lips don’t quite sync.”

Use Direction A as the base style.

Borrow only the layered contour technique from Phase 14A.1 Direction B /
Frame 2.

Do not copy Direction B’s entire paper-cut style.

Candidate A:

- One warm human
- One cool near-duplicate beside or slightly behind them
- Offset contour layers create subtle misregistration
- Head, face or upper-body alignment is almost correct but not fully resolved
- Clean Paper background
- No dark horror background

Candidate B:

- One warm human and one cool reflection-like echo
- Contour layers overlap through part of the composition
- The echo remains recognizably related to the human
- The visual uncertainty comes from registration and layering, not grotesque
  anatomy

Both candidates must avoid:

- Bars over the eyes
- Censorship symbolism
- Broken faces
- Melting bodies
- Glitches
- Video-call interfaces
- Phones
- Masks
- Horror treatment

CONCEPT 3 — MANUFACTURED CONSENSUS

Narrative meaning:

“Manufacturing consensus.”

Use Phase 14A.1 Direction A / Frame 4 as the visual foundation.

Candidate A:

- Many repeated synthetic human forms
- Forms progressively align and compress toward one shared visual output
- One restrained wave, band or signal-like form emerges
- Strong left-to-right editorial flow
- Human identities remain readable before becoming uniform

Candidate B:

- Many repeated synthetic forms arranged in organized layers
- Their separate positions resolve into one mechanically consistent direction
- Uniformity is communicated through synchronized posture, rhythm and spacing
- Composition remains broad and spacious rather than becoming a dense wall

Both candidates must avoid:

- Blank-eyed figures
- Masks
- Black central voids
- Propaganda-poster composition
- Giant arrows
- Interface panels
- Control dashboards
- Server racks
- Megaphones
- Platform logos
- Political symbols
