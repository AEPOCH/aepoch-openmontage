# ÆPOCH Series Bible

Governing document for the ÆPOCH video series. Every episode script, prompt set, and edit gets checked against this before it ships. If a creative decision isn't covered here, it goes back to the blog and social voice guide, the litepaper, and the Chris and Lee voice addenda before anyone improvises.

---

## 1. Editorial identity

### What the series exists to explain

The series exists to make one idea land: human presence has economic value, and right now the internet doesn't recognize that. Everything else — KAIROS, Proof of Life, Use or Lose, the DAO — is mechanism in service of that idea. An episode that explains a mechanism well but never connects it back to presence-as-value has missed its job.

### Intended viewer

Someone who already suspects something is off about how the internet treats them. They've felt the difference between being seen and being tracked. They don't need to be Bitcoin-literate. They don't need to know what a DAO is. They need to recognize the problem before they hear the name ÆPOCH.

Two viewer segments matter most: sovereignty-minded and Bitcoin-adjacent people who'll forgive more technical depth, and generally skeptical people who've been burned by crypto before and will leave at the first whiff of a pitch.

### Expected prior knowledge

None. Zero blockchain literacy assumed. If a script needs the viewer to already know what a wallet is, the script explains it in one sentence or cuts it. The Farmer Test from the website voice guide applies here too: a 40-year-old with no technical background should follow the core argument of every episode start to finish.

### Desired viewer reaction

Recognition, not persuasion. The target feeling is "I've thought this too, I just didn't have the words for it" — not "I've been convinced by an argument." A viewer who finishes an episode and goes looking for more information on their own terms is a win. A viewer who feels sold to has been failed by the episode, regardless of how accurate it was.

### Topics to avoid

- Price speculation or KAIROS valuation talk of any kind, even hedged. The litepaper's reference valuation is for internal planning only and has no place in viewer-facing content.
- Comparisons that position ÆPOCH as an investment opportunity. This is a presence protocol, not a financial product, and the video series doesn't blur that line even implicitly through pacing, music, or imagery.
- Direct attacks on named competitors or platforms. Presence-is-not-identity distinctions (World, Worldcoin, biometric ID systems) are fair territory when accurate and sourced, but the tone stays descriptive, never adversarial.
- Governance and DAO mechanics beyond what's true today. Epoch 1 governance is one human, one vote on the decisions that are actually live now — PoL parameter modifications, for example. Protocol upgrade governance doesn't open until Epoch 2. Don't imply a fuller governance picture than currently exists.
- Conspiratorial framing of existing financial or tech systems. Critique the system, name the mechanism, cite the source. Don't attribute malice without evidence.

### Claims requiring sourcing

Any claim that isn't clearly framed as ÆPOCH's own design philosophy needs a traceable source before it goes in a script:

- Historical or anthropological claims (rai stones, wampum, the history of money) — trace to the specific blog post or external source already vetted in the content library.
- Any statistic about surveillance, data brokers, platform economics, or the attention economy.
- Any comparison to World ID or other real-world biometric verification systems — these get fact-checked against current public information before the script locks, not assumed from memory.
- Any claim about what ÆPOCH's infrastructure currently does. The pilot runs on RSK testnet with Foundation-operated validators. That's the truth of today. Anything about ZK proof layers, uniqueness verification at scale, or decentralized governance is future vision and must be labeled as such on screen or in narration, not folded into a claim about present capability.

### Terminology that must remain exact

These are locked. No synonyms, no paraphrase, no "in other words":

| Term | Form | Never say instead |
|---|---|---|
| KAIROS | All caps | "the token," "the coin" |
| Kairos (as a countable unit) | Capitalized | lowercase "kairos" |
| Pulses | Sentence case | "cents," "sub-units" |
| Proof of Life | Title case | "proof of life" lowercase, "check-in" as a substitute term |
| Use or Lose (`pol_balance`) | Title case, no hyphens | "presence balance," "temporary balance," "expiring balance" |
| Yours to Keep (`free_balance`) | Title case, no hyphens | "permanent balance," "vault," "savings" |
| ÆPOCH | Always with the Æ ligature | "AEPOCH," "Epoch" (capital E, no ligature, refers to something else entirely) |
| Epoch (1, 2, 3...) | Capitalized, refers to the protocol's issuance phases | Don't confuse with the brand name ÆPOCH |
| Circulate | Preferred verb for spending/using KAIROS | "earn," "invest," "get paid" |
| Honorarium | Where applicable to any compensation context | "payment," "compensation," "reward" |

If a script needs to distinguish the current pilot from the eventual live protocol, say "the pilot" and "Epoch 1" as two different, non-interchangeable things. The pilot is not Epoch 1. Epoch 1 hasn't started.

## 2. Narrative voice

Intelligent but understandable. Visionary without sounding mystical or vague. Critical of existing systems without becoming conspiratorial. Human-centered rather than technology-centered. Specific rather than grandiose. Invitational rather than preachy.

In practice, this means:

The camera (literal or figurative) stays on people and consequences, not on architecture diagrams. When a mechanism has to be explained, it's explained through what it does to a person's day, not through its technical shape. "Every day you're real, you get one KAIROS" does the job. A slide about attestation flows doesn't.

Conviction reads through specificity, not through intensity. A script that says "this changes everything" has failed. A script that says "right now, a platform decides whether you're real by watching how long you hover over a button" has succeeded, because it's concrete enough to be checked and specific enough to be remembered.

The series never moralizes at the viewer. It doesn't tell them what kind of person to be. It names what a system does and invites them to imagine a different one. This is Lee's rule from the voice addendum and it holds here too: criticize the system, name the mechanism, leave the viewer's character out of it.

Humor, where it appears, is sparse and earned. It comes from an honest observation, not a joke inserted to relieve tension.

## 3. Episode rules

**One central idea per episode.** If a script is explaining two things, it's two episodes. Trying to cover Proof of Life and the Use or Lose mechanic in the same seven minutes means the viewer retains neither.

**One defined narrative question.** Every episode opens with a question the viewer already has, even if they haven't put words to it, and the episode's job is to sit with that question before answering it. "How does the internet know you're real?" is a narrative question. "Let's talk about ÆPOCH's architecture" is not.

**No unexplained tokenomics jargon.** If a term hasn't been defined in this episode or a prior one in the same series arc, it doesn't appear on screen or in narration without a plain-language gloss attached in the same breath.

**Define technical terms through human consequences.** A ZK proof isn't explained by describing zero-knowledge cryptography. It's explained by what it lets someone do: prove they're a real, unique person without handing over their face, their fingerprint, or their name. The mechanism serves the explanation of the consequence, not the other way around.

**Don't claim ÆPOCH solves problems that remain conceptual.** Uniqueness verification at scale and the ZK proof layer for Proof of Life are open technical problems right now, not shipped solutions. An episode can describe the vision. It cannot narrate the vision in the present tense as if it's already running.

**Distinguish existing implementation from future vision, on screen.** Every episode that touches how the protocol currently works needs a clear marker — visual, verbal, or both — for what's live in the pilot today versus what's designed for a later epoch. This isn't a caveat buried in fine print. It's part of the honest architecture of the episode.

**Avoid generic phrases such as "revolutionizing the future."** This extends to the whole family of empty intensifiers: game-changing, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, transformative, paradigm shift. If a sentence would still be true with the intensifier removed, remove it.

**Avoid cryptocurrency coin imagery.** No spinning coins, no coin stacks, no ticker-style number counters. KAIROS is not visualized as currency. If it needs a visual representation, it's tied to presence, time, or human activity, not to money iconography.

**Avoid hackers, hooded figures, glowing code, and cyberpunk cities.** This visual language belongs to a different genre and drags ÆPOCH into crypto-thriller aesthetics the series is explicitly trying to avoid. Visuals should look like the actual, ordinary world the viewer lives in.

**Avoid visual representations that imply guaranteed financial value.** No upward-trending charts implying price growth, no imagery suggesting KAIROS accumulation equals wealth. Circulation and presence get visualized. Financial upside doesn't.

## 4. Before an episode ships

Run every script against this before it goes to production:

1. Does the opening state a question the viewer already carries, without naming ÆPOCH first?
2. Is every locked term used exactly as defined in Part 1?
3. Is anything described as live today that's actually future vision? Mark it or cut it.
4. Would a viewer with zero crypto background follow this without pausing to look something up?
5. Does the episode make one argument, or is it quietly making two?
6. Does the closing land on a specific idea or open question, not a call to arms?
